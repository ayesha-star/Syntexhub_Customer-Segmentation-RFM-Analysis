# Customer Segmentation using RFM Analysis - Learning Notes

## Project Objective

The objective of this project is to analyze customer purchasing behavior using RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) Analysis. The project helps businesses identify valuable customers, understand customer behavior, and design targeted marketing strategies.

---

## Skills Learned

### SQL

- Created and managed a MySQL database.
- Imported CSV data into MySQL Workbench.
- Performed data validation and cleaning.
- Used aggregate functions like COUNT(), SUM(), MAX().
- Used GROUP BY for customer-level analysis.
- Used DATEDIFF() to calculate Recency.
- Used CASE statements to classify customers into different segments.

---

### Data Cleaning

Validated the dataset by checking:

- Missing (NULL) values
- Duplicate Transaction IDs
- Negative values
- Correct TotalAmount calculation
- Date range consistency
- Total customer count

These checks ensured the dataset was accurate before analysis.

---

### RFM Analysis

Learned the three important customer metrics:

#### Recency (R)

Measures how recently a customer made a purchase.

Smaller Recency means the customer is more active.

---

#### Frequency (F)

Measures how many purchases a customer has made.

Higher Frequency indicates a more engaged customer.

---

#### Monetary (M)

Measures the total amount spent by a customer.

Higher Monetary value indicates a more valuable customer.

---

### Customer Segmentation

Used RFM metrics to classify customers into four groups:

- Loyal Customer
- Regular Customer
- New Customer
- Churn Risk

This segmentation helps businesses personalize marketing campaigns.

---

### Business Analysis

Generated insights such as:

- Revenue generated by each customer segment
- Customer distribution across segments
- Revenue by product category
- Revenue by city
- Overall customer purchasing patterns

---

### Power BI

Built an interactive dashboard containing:

- KPI Cards
- Customer Segment Distribution
- Revenue by Customer Segment
- Customer RFM Analysis
- Revenue by Product Category
- Revenue by City

---

### Business Recommendations

Learned how data analysis supports business decisions.

Example:

- Reward loyal customers with exclusive benefits.
- Re-engage churn-risk customers through promotional campaigns.
- Encourage repeat purchases from regular customers.
- Build relationships with new customers through onboarding offers.
